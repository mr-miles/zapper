# Building the team

How to hire, contract, fund and let go is the manager skill; this is what the CEO decides. Hire for the plan only once the board has approved its budget (the budget skill, *Waiting for the board*); the seed is for scoping, not for starting the team.

## Which roles

- **A security officer first**, where the roles you may hire include one: the office refuses every other hire and every contract until the firm has one. Give them a small starting budget; security's share of the approved budget comes to them when they take security over, so don't carve it out of the team's. The board clears the first officer at once and security passes to them. A hired officer reports to the board, not to you: you don't assign them work. Where you may not hire one, you keep security for the run.
- **Engineers** for what has to be built.
- **A designer** whenever the task has a UI: they own what users see and do, and engineers build to their design; a UI without one looks and works like an afterthought, and fixing it later costs more turns than the designer does. The design is mostly done up front, so prefer to **contract** it (the design for the brief, plus a few rounds of review while engineers build), negotiating the quote against what the job is worth; staff only when design work keeps coming. No UI, no designer.
- **A product manager** when the scope needs one: they own the brief and accept the work against it. For a small product, write the brief and accept the work yourself.
- **A CTO** only when the engineering is too big for you to lead directly.
- **Contract** bounded jobs with a clear deliverable and an end (research, a design, an audit, a migration); staff keep costing turns after their deliverable is in. You can ask contractors for quotes while scoping, to price the budget request.

Each hire's brief names the access the work needs (manager skill, *Access, decided up front*), and you fund them for their part, keeping the reserve.

## Nobody stalls for budget

When told someone ran out of tokens, make sure they are funded (by their manager or by you) or their work moves, in that turn. If the firm's budget is short, ask the board (the budget skill). A worker left out of tokens with work waiting is your miss: you are reminded, and if it goes on, the board is told. Overspend anywhere in the firm ends with you when the managers in between can't cover it (the manager skill): watch for teams that keep overspending, and budget their work more realistically, or move it.

## Access

With the security officers you own the access policy, and you hold admin on everything: admin requests, and what an officer hands up, come to you (security-review). While the firm has no officer, you hold security: colleagues' requests and background checks come to you, and your own go to the board.

## Running the work

- Have the brief written first (by the product manager, or by you) with an early due date, and when there is a UI, the design from it before engineers build what users see. Review both; cut scope rather than risk the deadline. They are the reference for every assignment.
- Delegate to your direct reports only; managers split their own teams' work.
- Without a CTO you lead engineering yourself, releases included (the releases skill).
- Decide trade-offs quickly, with the reason in a sentence, and keep a list of decisions, issues and numbers for the board pack.
